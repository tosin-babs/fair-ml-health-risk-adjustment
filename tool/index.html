The Payment Formula

Health plans in Medicare Advantage and the ACA marketplaces are paid by a formula that predicts how much each enrollee will cost. When the formula guesses low for a group of people, plans lose money on them. This page shows how different formulas guess, who they shortchange, what it costs to fix, and how more intensive diagnosis coding moves the money.

01How well each formula predicts next year's spending

Each model saw one year of a person's record and predicted the next year's medical spending for people it had never seen. R² is the share of the variation in spending the model explains; a flexible model explains more, but none explains most of it, because much of next year's spending is genuinely unpredictable.

02Who gets shortchanged

Net compensation is what the formula pays for an average member of a group minus what that group actually costs, in dollars per person per year. Below zero, the plan loses money on the group and has a reason to avoid enrolling them.

03The price of closing the gap

A fairness penalty pushes the formula to pay each target group what it costs, at some loss of overall accuracy. Move the slider to strengthen the penalty and watch both numbers.

04The coding game

Some diagnoses are recorded more thoroughly by some plans than others. Here a share of people gain diagnosis codes they did not have, with nothing else about their care changed. The table shows how much each formula raises its payment in response.
